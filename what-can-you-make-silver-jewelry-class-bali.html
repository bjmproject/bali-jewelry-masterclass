JEWELRY MAKING IN BALI

What Can You Make in a Silver Jewelry Class in Bali?
Rings, Pendants, Bracelets & More

You know you want to make silver jewelry.

The next question is obvious:

What can you actually make?

A ring is a natural starting point, but it does not have to be the only possibility.

Depending on the workshop and the design, you may also be able to make or discuss ideas for pendants, bracelets, earrings, or other pieces.

The important word is design.

A simple pendant and an elaborate pendant are two very different projects. The same is true for rings, bracelets, and earrings.

So the more useful question is not simply:

“Can I make a bracelet?”

It is:

“Can I make this bracelet within the available material, time, and techniques?”

Here is how to think about the possibilities.

Rings

Rings are a popular choice for a jewelry-making workshop because a relatively straightforward form still leaves plenty of room for variation.

You can change the:

  • width
  • thickness
  • proportions
  • shape
  • texture
  • surface finish
  • small decorative details

A narrow polished band can look completely different from a wider ring with visible hammer texture.

And two rings based on the same general idea can still feel different once their proportions and surfaces change.

Ring size also affects the design.

A larger or more substantial ring generally requires more material than a smaller or lighter one, so the amount of silver needed should be considered before the making begins.

Pendants

Pendants offer a different kind of design freedom because they do not need to fit around a finger.

The outline itself can become a major part of the design.

You might prefer something geometric, something more organic, or a relatively simple form where texture becomes the main visual feature.

But a pendant is not automatically easier than a ring.

A simple shape may be practical for a workshop, while a design involving several components, very fine details, or specialized techniques may require considerably more work.

Size matters too.

A larger pendant can require more silver even when its shape appears simple.

Bracelets

Bracelets introduce another practical consideration: scale.

Because a bracelet needs to fit around the wrist, some designs can require considerably more material than a ring.

But again, the jewelry category alone does not tell you how difficult the project will be.

A relatively simple bracelet form and a design made from multiple detailed components may require very different amounts of time, material, and technical work.

If you want to make a bracelet, the specific design should be considered before the session.

That allows the material requirement and practicality of the idea to be checked before you begin.

Earrings

Earrings can also be worth discussing, but they need slightly different planning.

A design may involve one piece or multiple components, and a matching pair introduces additional considerations such as size, symmetry, attachments, material, and time.

At Bali Jewelry Masterclass, the standard workshop includes one finished piece per guest, so do not assume that a pair of earrings is automatically included as one standard piece.

If earrings are what you would like to make, discuss the exact design and whether you want a single piece or pair before the workshop.

The team can then consider what the design requires and explain any additional material, time, components, or work before you begin.

Can you bring your own design?

Yes, you can bring an idea or reference to discuss.

A photo on your phone is often the easiest way to show what you have in mind.

But a reference image is best treated as a starting point rather than a guarantee that the piece can be reproduced exactly.

The original jewelry may use:

  • more material than is available in the standard workshop
  • techniques outside the workshop format
  • specialized tools
  • multiple components
  • gemstones or other materials
  • construction that requires more time
  • details that are difficult to reproduce at a beginner level

That does not necessarily mean abandoning the idea.

Often the part you like most can be identified and adapted.

Maybe it is the overall shape.

Maybe it is the width.

Maybe it is the texture or finish.

Keeping those elements while simplifying something else can make a reference much more practical for a workshop.

What makes a design more complicated?

Visual complexity and technical complexity are not always the same thing.

A piece that looks minimal can still be difficult to make.

And a visually distinctive piece can sometimes be created from a relatively straightforward form.

Things that can affect complexity include:

  • the number of separate components
  • very fine details
  • difficult or precise shapes
  • symmetry requirements
  • the amount of silver required
  • multiple joins or soldering stages
  • gemstones or stone settings
  • specialized finishing
  • additional materials
  • techniques requiring greater instructor involvement

This is why a photo alone cannot always tell you whether a design is practical for a workshop.

Someone needs to consider how it would actually be made.

How much does silver weight affect what you can make?

Quite a lot.

Jewelry occupies physical space, and larger or heavier designs need more material.

Width, thickness, length, ring size, and the overall construction of the piece can all affect the amount of silver required.

At Bali Jewelry Masterclass, the standard workshop includes up to 6 grams of silver per guest.

That may be suitable for many designs, but it is not a promise that every ring, pendant, bracelet, or earring idea will fit within that allowance.

If your design requires more silver, additional material is available at IDR 95,000 per gram.

The required amount should be discussed before the additional silver is used.

What if your idea is too complicated?

You may still have options.

A design can sometimes be adapted without losing the part that made you like it in the first place.

A large piece might be made smaller.

A heavy form might be made lighter.

Several details might be reduced to one.

A difficult construction might be simplified while keeping a similar overall character.

In other cases, the piece may require additional time, material, special work, or greater instructor involvement.

And occasionally an idea may simply not suit the workshop format.

That is useful information to have before you begin.

If the design matters to you, send or bring the reference and discuss what is realistic.

Do beginners have fewer options?

Being a beginner does not automatically restrict you to one category of jewelry.

The specific design matters more.

A straightforward pendant may be more approachable than a complicated ring.

A simple ring may be more practical than an intricate earring design.

The aim is not to choose the easiest category.

It is to choose a design that gives you a meaningful role in making the piece while remaining realistic for the available session.

Your instructor can help you find that balance.

How should you decide what to make?

Start with what you would genuinely like to wear or keep.

Then narrow the idea down.

Ask yourself:

  1. What type of jewelry do I want?
  2. What shape or proportions do I like?
  3. Do I want a smooth surface or visible texture?
  4. Is there one detail that matters most to me?
  5. Am I willing to adapt the design if it requires too much material or time?

You do not need to solve the technical side yourself.

That is what the design discussion with your instructor is for.

Your job is to know what you like.

Then the idea can be translated into something practical to make.

What can you make at Bali Jewelry Masterclass?

Rings are the main focus at Bali Jewelry Masterclass, but other jewelry ideas can be discussed.

Depending on the design, this may include pieces such as:

  • pendants
  • bracelets
  • earrings
  • other custom jewelry ideas

Whether a particular piece is practical depends on its size, material requirement, techniques, complexity, and the time needed to make it.

The standard workshop includes:

  • Two hours of workshop time
  • Up to 6 grams of silver per guest
  • One finished piece per guest
  • Tools and equipment
  • Instructor guidance

No previous jewelry-making experience is required.

If your idea requires additional silver, time, special materials, or other work beyond the standard workshop, the requirement and estimated cost will be explained before that extra work begins.

For anything outside a straightforward ring design, sharing a reference before your session is the easiest way to start the conversation.

Start with the idea, then make it workable

There is no useful master list of every piece of jewelry that can or cannot be made in a workshop.

A category tells you what something is.

It does not tell you how difficult it is to make.

A ring can be simple or complicated.

So can a pendant.

So can a bracelet.

So can an earring design.

Start with the piece you actually want.

Then look at the design itself: its size, material, construction, details, and the time required.

Sometimes your original idea will work as it is.

Sometimes it will need adapting.

Either way, the goal is not to squeeze the most complicated possible project into the session.

It is to make something you want to keep—and to have enough room in the process to actually take part in making it.
